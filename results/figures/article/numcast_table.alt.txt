Table of the effect over base of ag_default, skrub_tv, base_numcast (base with number-like categorical columns cast to float) and base_codes (base with every categorical column as integer codes) on fps_benchmark, auction_verification, Brazilian_houses, socmob and Amazon_employee_access, one row per dataset and downstream model, in % RMSE or AUC points. With CatBoost, ag_default, which keeps categorical columns as categories (dropping or retyping some), is within 3.4% of base on all five datasets. skrub_tv, which turns every column into numbers, gains 51.2%, 68.8% and 20.7% RMSE on auction_verification, fps_benchmark and Brazilian_houses, and base_codes gains 49.4%, 60.2% and 35.1%; base_numcast reproduces the auction_verification gain (+49.4%) but gives only +2.3% on fps_benchmark and +15.4% on Brazilian_houses. On Amazon_employee_access numbers cost 3.0 to 5.3 AUC points. With LightGBM base_codes loses 59.7% on fps_benchmark and 14.0% on socmob, and gains 19.9% on auction_verification. Green cells mark a clearly positive effect, red cells a clearly negative one (the bootstrap interval over the splits excludes zero).
